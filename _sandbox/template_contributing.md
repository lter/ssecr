# Intellectual Credit Guidelines for XXX Project

This project had its genesis in the LTER Synthesis Skills for Early Career Researchers course of 2026. The original project group [insert names here] developed the idea, gathered data... [fill in here however far you have gotten/expect to get in the course]. These guidelines articulate how we, as a group, have agreed to share intellectual credit for project outputs both during the course and in any related project work that may take place after the course has ended.

_The headings and prompts below are intended to guide and facilitate substantive group discussion, not to constrain group decision making. There is no wrong way to do this except to avoid the conversation._ 

## Core Products

Core products are those directly underpinned by the course-related research question, data acquisition, and processing.
_What is your group definition of a core product?_ 
_What type of contribution warrants authorship of core products? Is group membership sufficient?_ 
_Are there different requirements for data products than papers?_
_How will you determine author order? Note that different disciplines have different conventions regarding the meaning of author order. be sure you understand how it will be interpreted in the relevant fields and journals._
_If you use a [CReDiT](https://doi.org/10.1087/20150211) or other framework for documenting contributions, this is a good place to link it._

### Leaving the Team

The course commitment does not imply a commitment to continue working on the project after the conclusion of classes. Some group members may want to step away from the project at that time.
_How should they communicate their intention?_
_Does course participation imply a minimum authorship credit? If so, what? If not, what else is required?_
_If they later want to re-engage, what is the process?_

### Joining the Team

Synthesis teams are rarely static. If you continue with this project after the course, you will very likely want to recruit additional team members.
_How should they indicate their interest? What level of commitment or effort will you expect? How will that be communicated?_
_Are authorship requirements the same for newcomers as for existing team members?_
_Where do they fit in the author order?_

## Supplemental Products

Supplemental (or spin-off) products come from ideas that may have been floated, but not pursued, during the class. You may want to treat them differently than core products (or you may not).
_Discuss and document what constitutes a supplemental product and what your process is for opting into (or out of) them._
_Are authorship expectations the same or different for supplemental products?_
_Is dataset contribution sufficient to warrant authorship on supplemental products?_ 
_In addition to the above questions, one important consideration for supplemental products is whether original group members have the opportunity to opt-in to them and, if so, how they will hear about the opportunity to participate in new products._

## Funding

Synthesis projects can unfold over years and it is easy to lose track of what entities provided support along the way. The process you use for tracking contributions can often be extended to include financial support. Discuss what types of financial support are relevant and how you will document them.
_Consider different types of support including salary, convening, logistical, analytical..._
_Recrd the relevant grant numbers and preferred citation while they are easily available. They can be hard to track down later. For example, NCEAS [recommends specific language](https://www.nceas.ucsb.edu/acknowledging-nceas-support) for each of its programs. SSECR products should use the LTER language, but reference the SSECR course rather than a working group name._
