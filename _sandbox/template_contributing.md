These guidelines articulate how we, as a group, have agreed to share intellectual credit for project outputs both during the course and in any related project work that may take place after the course has ended.

*The headings and prompts below are intended to guide and facilitate substantive group discussion, *not* to constrain group decision making. There is no wrong way to do this except to avoid the conversation.*

## Core Products

Core products are those directly underpinned by the course-related research question, data acquisition, and processing.

- *What is your group definition of a core product?*
- *What type of contribution warrants authorship of core products? Is group membership sufficient?*
- *Are there different requirements for data products than papers?* *How will you determine author order?*
    - Note that different disciplines have different conventions regarding the meaning of author order. Be sure you understand how it will be interpreted in the relevant fields and journals

- *If you use a [CRediT](https://doi.org/10.1087/20150211) or other framework for documenting contributions, this is a good place to link it.*

## Supplemental Products

Supplemental (or spin-off) products come from ideas that may have been floated, but not pursued, during the class. You may want to treat them differently than core products (or you may not).

- *Discuss and document what constitutes a supplemental product and what your process is for opting into (or out of) them*
- *Are authorship expectations the same or different for supplemental products?*
- *Is dataset contribution sufficient to warrant authorship on supplemental products?*
- *In addition to the above questions, one important consideration for supplemental products is whether original group members have the opportunity to opt-in to them and, if so, how they will hear about the opportunity to participate in new products*

## Team Turnover

Synthesis teams are rarely static. If you continue with this project after the course, you will very likely want to recruit additional team members. Additionally, it is fully possible that current members may want to step back (or even step back and eventually jump back in!).

## _Leaving_ the Team

- *How should they communicate their intention?*
- *Does course participation imply a minimum level of formal credit? If so, what? If not, what else is required?*
- *If they later want to re-engage, what is the process?*

## _Joining_ the Team

- *How should they indicate their interest?*
- *What level of commitment or effort will you expect? How will that be communicated?*
- *Are authorship requirements the same for newcomers as for existing team members?* *Where do they fit in the author order?*

## Funding

Synthesis projects can unfold over years and it is easy to lose track of what entities provided support along the way. The process you use for tracking contributions can often be extended to include financial support. Discuss what types of financial support are relevant and how you will document them.

- *Consider different types of support including salary, convening, logistical, analytical...* *Record the relevant grant numbers and preferred citation while they are easily available. They can be hard to track down later*
    - For example, NCEAS [recommends specific language](https://www.nceas.ucsb.edu/acknowledging-nceas-support) for each of its programs
    SSECR products should use the LTER language, but reference the SSECR course rather than a working group name
